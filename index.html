Abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies trained on demonstrations from a fixed camera depend brittly on the camera that observes the scene at deployment. We propose to regularize the action-flow field directly, using action-equivalent multi-view pair data: two observations of the same physical state with shared language, proprioception, and demonstrated action.

We introduce Multi-Sample Bilateral Cross-View Action-Flow Consistency with Action-Biased Time Sampling: for each pair, K = 2 flow-time and noise samples are drawn with t ∼ Beta(2, 3) to concentrate supervision in the action-near regime, the action-flow field is evaluated on both views, and the two predictions are pulled toward each other by a bilateral cross-view loss in which both branches receive gradient (no stop-gradient on either side), while standard flow matching is preserved on both views. A natural-looking stop-gradient asymmetric variant turns out to regress deployment robustness: it lets the nominal branch escape the mutual constraint and the perturbed branch chase a moving target, which we record as an ablation.

A mechanism analysis shows that the matched bilateral cross-view loss upper-bounds the action divergence between deployed nominal- and perturbed-view trajectories, while a shuffled-pair variant carries no view-disagreement signal; the corresponding shuffled-pair ablation confirms this prediction empirically. The deployed policy receives only a single RGB scene image, a language instruction, and a proprioceptive robot state (the wrist channel is masked throughout to make the scene-view robustness claim well-defined).

Our method achieves 86.9% on the LIBERO-Plus camera-perturbation track (4,170 / 4,797, Wilson 95% CI [85.9, 87.9]), outperforming the strongest zero-shot transfer baseline (π0.5 at 75.8%, ACoT-VLA at 72.6%) by more than 11pp, and outperforming a naive-mixing baseline trained on the LIBERO-Plus public training distribution (74.7%) by +12.2pp — the central evidence that action-equivalent cross-view consistency provides a mechanism beyond raw camera-augmented training data. Methods reporting higher camera-track scores rely on architectural changes or LIBERO-Plus-augmented supervised fine-tuning; our contribution is the consistency mechanism, which preserves the single-view inference contract. We also report a synchronized multi-camera real-robot evaluation.

Method

Standard VLA training produces a policy that maps a single observation (RGB scene image, language instruction, proprioception) to a distribution over action chunks via a learned action-flow field. When the camera viewpoint changes at deployment, the same physical state produces a different image, and the unregularized flow field may produce a different action. We address this directly.

Figure 1: Multi-Sample Bilateral Cross-View Action-Flow Consistency. For each same-state nominal/perturbed view pair, K=2 flow-time/noise samples are drawn; the action-flow predictions from both views are pulled toward each other by a bilateral loss (both branches receive gradient), while standard flow matching is preserved.

Core objective. For a same-state view pair (oa, ob) with shared language l, proprioception q, and demonstrated action a:

ℒtotal = ℒFM(oa) + ℒFM(ob) + λ · ℒCV

where ℒCV = (1/K) ∑k ||vθ(xt, t | oa, l, q) − vθ(xt, t | ob, l, q)||2 with t ∼ Beta(2, 3) and no stop-gradient on either branch.

Key design choices

  • Bilateral gradient (no stop-gradient). Both views are mutually constrained. Stop-gradient on one branch (asymmetric) lets that branch over-specialize to action-fitting without view-invariance pressure and causes the other to chase a moving target — our B6 ablation regresses full LIBERO-Plus by 0.6pp despite improving in-distribution accuracy.
  • Action-biased time sampling [Beta(2, 3) vs. legacy Beta(1.5, 1)]. Concentrates cross-view supervision in the action-near flow regime (t ≈ 0) where precise visual disambiguation matters most. Camera orientation changes (C3) benefit most (+2.7pp), consistent with theory.
  • Multi-sample averaging (K=2). Reduces gradient variance from a single (t, ε) slice, yielding more stable cross-view supervision than single-sample K=1.
  • No spatial augmentation between paired views. Independent spatial augmentations applied to each view of a pair introduce inconsistent geometric transforms that corrupt the action-equivalent pairing guarantee. View-consistent pair construction is essential (A2 ablation confirms).

Results

We evaluate on the LIBERO-Plus camera-perturbation benchmark (4,797 trials across three perturbation conditions: C1 camera distance/scale, C2 spherical position, C3 camera orientation). All evaluations use the wrist-camera channel masked black; the policy receives only a single RGB scene image, language instruction, and proprioceptive state at deployment.
