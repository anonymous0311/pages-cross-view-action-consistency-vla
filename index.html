Abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies fine-tuned from a fixed scene camera can fail when the camera is moved, even when the task, objects, language, and robot state are unchanged. We study scene-camera viewpoint robustness under a strict deployment interface: a single scene RGB image, language, and proprioception, with no camera label, extrinsics, depth, point cloud, wrist image, or multi-view input. For flow-based VLAs, we propose to regularize the action-flow velocity field across action-equivalent views. We construct action-equivalent view pairs by resetting original LIBERO demonstrations to the same MuJoCo state and rendering nominal and perturbed scene-camera views. Both views are supervised by flow matching, while a cross-view loss encourages their predicted action-flow velocities to agree at the same sampled flow coordinates.

On the LIBERO-Plus camera-perturbation track (4,797 trials), our method reaches 87.2 ± 0.4% over three seeds, +7.7 pp over flow-matching-only training on the same paired data and +12.5 pp over naive mixed-camera SFT, while preserving nominal-camera in-distribution performance (95.0 ± 0.8%). A shuffled-pair control collapses to 25.8%, showing that the gain depends on action-equivalent pairing.

Method: Cross-View Action-Flow Consistency

Key idea 1

Action-flow consistency, not feature matching. The cross-view loss acts on the velocity field rather than a hidden feature. Feature agreement does not guarantee action agreement, while final-action matching would entangle velocity disagreement with integration effects.

Key idea 2

Bilateral gradient flow. Both the nominal and perturbed branch receive the cross-view gradient, mutually pulling their predictions toward a shared view-invariant action-flow space. One-sided stop-gradient gives weaker robustness.

Key idea 3

Action-equivalent pairing is load-bearing. Shuffling the pairing keeps per-view flow-matching labels correct but destroys state–action correspondence inside the cross-view term. The shuffled control collapses to 25.8%, isolating matched pairing as the mechanism.

Key idea 4

Zero inference overhead. The paired structure is used only at training time. The deployed policy is identical to the base VLA: one RGB scene image + language + proprioception — no camera labels, depth, or multi-view input.

Real-Robot Held-Out Camera Evaluation

We evaluate on two tabletop tasks — Pick & Place (blue battery into cardboard box) and Contact Rich (closing a laptop lid) — with 10 rollouts per task, camera placement, and method. Each policy is tested from both a seen training-camera placement and a held-out camera placement never used during data collection.
